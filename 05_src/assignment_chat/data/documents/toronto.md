# Toronto City Guide

Toronto is the capital of Ontario and a major Canadian city located on the northwestern shore of Lake Ontario.

## Downtown Attractions

The CN Tower, Ripley's Aquarium of Canada, St. Lawrence Market, and the Distillery District are popular downtown attractions.

The CN Tower and Ripley's Aquarium are close to Union Station and can reasonably be visited during the same part of the day.

St. Lawrence Market is known for food vendors, specialty products, and local cuisine. The Distillery District contains pedestrian streets, historic buildings, restaurants, shops, and galleries.

## Nature and Outdoor Activities

Toronto Islands offer beaches, walking paths, cycling routes, picnic areas, and views of the Toronto skyline. Ferries depart from the Jack Layton Ferry Terminal.

High Park is a large urban park with walking trails, gardens, picnic areas, sports facilities, and seasonal events.

The waterfront contains walking and cycling paths near Lake Ontario.

## Transportation

The Toronto Transit Commission operates subway, streetcar, and bus services.

Union Station is Toronto's main transportation hub for subway, regional rail, airport trains, and intercity rail services.

Visitors can use public transportation for most major downtown attractions. Walking is also practical in areas such as the waterfront, the entertainment district, and the financial district.

## Neighbourhoods

Kensington Market is known for independent businesses, vintage shops, cafes, and diverse food.

The Distillery District is known for historic architecture and pedestrian streets.

Yorkville contains boutiques, restaurants, galleries, and higher-end shopping.

## Sample Planning Advice

Downtown attractions should be grouped together to reduce travel time. A visit to the Toronto Islands should be given several hours because it requires a ferry journey.
