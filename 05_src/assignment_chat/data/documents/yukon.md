# Yukon Travel Guide

Yukon is Canada's westernmost territory and is known for its vast wilderness, mountain landscapes, northern lights, wildlife, and outdoor adventure opportunities. Whitehorse is the territorial capital and the largest community in Yukon.

## Whitehorse Attractions

The SS Klondike National Historic Site showcases one of the historic sternwheelers that once operated along the Yukon River.

The Yukon Wildlife Preserve allows visitors to observe native northern animals such as moose, woodland caribou, muskoxen, mountain goats, wood bison, and Arctic foxes.

The MacBride Museum of Yukon History presents exhibits about Indigenous cultures, the Klondike Gold Rush, and the history of the territory.

Miles Canyon offers walking trails, scenic viewpoints, and access to the Yukon River.

## Outdoor Activities

Yukon is popular for hiking, canoeing, fishing, camping, wildlife photography, and mountain exploration.

Kluane National Park and Reserve is home to Canada's highest mountain, Mount Logan, along with glaciers, hiking trails, and scenic drives.

The Yukon River provides opportunities for canoeing and guided river excursions during warmer months.

Winter activities include dog sledding, snowmobiling, cross-country skiing, snowshoeing, and ice fishing.

## Northern Lights

Yukon is considered one of Canada's best destinations for viewing the Aurora Borealis.

The northern lights are generally visible during clear nights between late summer and early spring, with reduced light pollution improving viewing conditions.

Guided northern-lights tours are available from Whitehorse during the winter season.

## Transportation

Whitehorse International Airport connects Yukon with several Canadian cities.

Personal vehicles are commonly used for exploring the territory because many attractions are separated by long driving distances.

Guided tours are available for visitors who prefer organized transportation.

Road conditions may vary depending on weather, especially during winter.

## Culture and History

Yukon has a rich Indigenous heritage and played an important role during the Klondike Gold Rush.

Dawson City preserves many historic buildings and museums connected to the gold rush era and remains one of Yukon's most visited destinations.

## Sample Planning Advice

Whitehorse attractions can usually be explored within one or two days.

Visitors interested in the northern lights should plan multiple evenings to improve their chances of clear viewing conditions.

Trips to Kluane National Park require additional travel time and should be planned as a separate full-day excursion from Whitehorse.
